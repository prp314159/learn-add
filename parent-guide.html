Parent Guide

A few simple ways to help your child understand adding, taking away and groups. Sit with him for the first sessions and talk while he plays.

🌟 Always

  • Real things first: cars, blocks, grapes. The screen comes after.
  • Short and happy: ten minutes is plenty. A trophy is a good moment to stop.
  • Ask "how did you know?" Any answer is good. Explaining is where learning settles.
  • A mistake is a clue, not a failure. Count it again together, slowly.

➕ Adding

Two groups come together. How many are there now?

🚗🚗🚗and🚙🚙make5

Say

  • "3 and 2 make 5."

Ask

  • "How many altogether?"
  • "What if one more came?"

Away from the screen

  • "You have 3 cars. I give you 2 more. How many cars now?"

➖ Taking away

Some go away. How many are left?

🍇🍇🍇🍇🍇leaves3

Say

  • "5 take away 2 leaves 3."

Ask

  • "How many are left?"
  • "What if one more went away?"

Away from the screen

  • Five fingers up, fold two down.
  • "You have 5 grapes and eat 2." And: "You eat all 3. How many left? None!"

Watch for

  • If he answers 2 (the ones that went), point to the ones still there and ask "how many are left?"

✖️ Groups

The same group, again and again. 3 × 2 means 3 plates with 2 on each.

make6

Say

  • "3 plates. 2 on each. 6 altogether."
  • Later: "3 groups of 2 make 6."

Ask

  • "How many plates? How many on each?"
  • "What if one more plate came?"

Away from the screen

  • Wheels on cars, socks in pairs, legs on chairs. Always ask "how many on each?"

On paper

  • Draw the circles first, then put dots in each, then count them all.
  • Much later, a big challenge: 3 × 4. Three circles with 4 dots each is a pass.

Watch for

  • If he says 5 for 3 × 2, he is adding. Ask again: "how many plates? how many on each?"
Go slowly. Understanding comes before speed.
